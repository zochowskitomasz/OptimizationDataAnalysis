Furthermore, we check the importance of using burn-in and warmup techniques for gradient stabilization during the initial phase of training. We expand on the original authors' paper by evaluating performance of these methods for a smaller transformer model.

\section{Introduction}

Stochastic gradient descent is considered a simple, robust, and universal optimization algorithm that can be used for various machine learning tasks. In expectation, it finds (local) optimal points for both convex and non-convex functions. Furthermore, if we assume bounded gradient variance, the algorithm has a definite convergence rate; if we also assume that the distribution of gradients has light tails, SGD with momentum works with high probability.

These assumptions, however, are not certain. It turns out that, particularly in transformers, the gradients have heavy tails and their variance is practically infinite. There are certain methods utilized to fight these unfavourable properties, one of them being gradient clipping. Moreover, momentum helps with gradient stabilization, which makes the algorithm more robust.

In \cite{heavy-tails} authors describe these methods and propose two algorithms that employ them and achieve satisfying convergence rates in high probability. Furthermore, they introduce burn-in -- a process of skipping initial training steps before the gradient is accumulated -- and compare it to widely known warmup technique.

Our goal is to implement these algorithms and compare them to the Adam optimizer -- a state-of-the-art solution for neural networks. Moreover, we check how burn-in and warmup strategies work for all three optimizers mentioned. We expand on authors' results by applying these algorithms and learning rate manipulation strategies to much smaller transformers than BERT-Large.
